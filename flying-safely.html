Flying safely and legally

A home-built drone of about half a kilo comes with real rules in the UK. Here's what they are, and how I test without putting anyone at risk.

The rules

A privately built drone over 250 g flies in the CAA's A3 "far from people" category. That means:

  • At least 150 m from houses, parks, shops and industrial areas
  • At least 50 m from anyone not involved in the flight
  • Never higher than 120 m
  • A Flyer ID for the pilot and an Operator ID on the drone
  • No flying near airfields or restricted zones, including military sites

What that means for me

I have my Flyer ID, and my supervisor, who's more experienced, holds the Operator ID.

A garden counts as a residential area, so no free flight at home. At home the drone is always held: on a thrust stand, or in a gimbal rig where it can tilt but not leave.

Real flights happen at a proper flying site, through FPV UK membership or a model-flying club field. Before every session I check the airspace map, nearby restrictions and the wind.

Test procedure

The steps every session follows, in order.

  1. 1

    Kill button first

    Before anything else in every session, I test that the kill button stops the motors.

  2. 2

    Props off on the bench

    All bench work happens without propellers. Any new board is powered up for the first time through a smoke stopper, which limits the current if I've made a mistake.

  3. 3

    Props on only when held

    Propellers go on only for the thrust stand, the gimbal rig, or at the flying site with my supervisor holding the kill switch.

  4. 4

    Pre-flight check

    Airspace checked, frame screws tight, props tight and the right way round, battery strapped down, SD card in and logging.

  5. 5

    Start low

    The first flights stay under 2 m, over grass, in light wind, with everyone at least 10 m back.

  6. 6

    Read the logs

    After every flight I download the log and look at the data before changing anything.

Batteries

LiPo batteries store a lot of energy and can catch fire if they're damaged or mistreated.

  • Always charged inside a LiPo-safe bag, on a non-flammable surface, never left alone
  • Stored at half charge (3.8 V per cell) if they won't be used for a few days
  • A swollen ("puffed") battery is never used again

Coming to watch a flight? Stay behind me and at least 10 m from the drone, and keep pets and kids back too. If I shout "kill", the motors are being stopped and that's normal.
